StatsCo AI

Live app · Python, SQL, LLMs, sentence-transformers, Streamlit

A chat app that answers questions about Colombia’s official statistics, in English or Spanish. Ask “What was the unemployment rate in Antioquia in 2023?” and it replies with the figure and the datasets it came from — or with a chart or a department map, if you ask for one.

  • A first model call screens out off-topic, unclear or malicious questions before any data is touched.
  • Sentence embeddings pick the relevant tables from a 1.8 GB SQLite database, refreshed twice a month from DANE, Banco de la República, MinHacienda, the World Bank and Migración Colombia.
  • The schema the model reads comes from the database itself: every table stores its own descriptions, units and allowed values.
  • Generated SQL runs on a read-only connection with a row cap. If a query fails or returns nothing, the model gets the error back and tries again, up to three times.
  • Per-user and global daily limits put a ceiling on API costs.

Statistics on Colombia

Live app · Python, pandas, Plotly, Streamlit

A public dashboard of Colombia’s economy, poverty and population, built from the raw files each agency publishes. Every source formats its data differently, so each one has its own cleaning script that turns the original workbook into a tidy table the app reads.

It covers GDP from three approaches, inflation by city and spending category, the job market, productivity, public debt and the fiscal deficit, poverty and inequality, births, deaths and migration. Any series can be lined up by presidential term on a shared year-of-term axis, so administrations are compared at the same point in their terms.

AniDash and its score predictor

Live app · Python, scikit-learn, rapidfuzz, GitHub Actions, Streamlit

Turns a personal anime watch list into a nine-page dashboard, and estimates how well a show will score before it airs.

  • Titles are matched against more than 14,000 anime: exact match first, then fuzzy matching at 85% similarity within compatible formats, so a series and a film with the same name stay apart.
  • A GitHub Actions job pulls airing shows from the AniList API every day, and the full season every Monday.
  • The score model (gradient boosting) only sees information that exists before release; popularity, members and favourites were left out to avoid leakage. On 298 titles from 2026 that it never saw, its error is 7.3 points RMSE on a 0–100 scale, against 12.8 for always predicting the average.

Are there really that many isekai?

Written analysis · pandas, NumPy, Matplotlib, Jupyter

Anime fans often complain that the isekai genre has flooded the market. I tested the claim.

  • Releases grew 1,020% between 2000–04 and 2020–24: an 11.0% compound annual rate, against 6.3% for anime overall.
  • Release counts follow light-novel releases from two years earlier (r = 0.96), which suggests the novels feed the adaptations.
  • An IQR outlier test shows isekai’s share is not abnormal; school settings stand out more.

The growth is real, but the genre has joined the top tier rather than broken it. The methodology and its limitations are written up next to the code.
